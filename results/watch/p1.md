# P1 — SPR exchange-return performance

**Supplementary evidence log. Post-freeze, descriptive only.**

**Resolves 2027-12-31.**

## The frozen definition governs

From [`../../prereg/slate-v1.1.md`](../../prereg/slate-v1.1.md) §1.

**YES** if at least **50%** of the exchange-agreement crude due for physical
return to the SPR on or before 2027-12-31 **under the initial DOE-published
return schedule for each 2026 exchange agreement** has been physically delivered
by that date.

**The denominator is anchored to the initial schedule.** Subsequent
renegotiations, deferrals or amended schedules do not change it. **Deferral of
initially-due crude counts as non-delivery.**

That anchor exists because deferral is the thesis's own predicted behaviour. A
denominator that shrank with each deferral would convert the thesis's world into
a YES for the null.

**Zero-denominator guard:** if no exchange crude was due on or before
2027-12-31 under initial schedules, P1 resolves **UNSCORED** with annotation.

**P(YES):** thesis 0.30, null 0.70.

### Third attempt, 2026-08-21 - the artefact exists, and it is the SOLICITATION

The two earlier attempts searched for a published *schedule*. This one searched
the remaining avenue the file itself listed: contemporaneous trade press. It did
not produce the anchor, but it located where the anchor lives, which changes the
FOIA question from a guess into a targeted request.

**DOE's own announcement carries no return dates.** The RFP article of
2026-06-10 was read directly. It gives the volume (up to 40 million barrels), a
26 percent return premium, and the sites (Big Hill and Bryan Mound). It states
no return date, no window, and no per-agreement schedule. This is consistent
with both earlier negatives rather than a new result.

**Trade and analyst coverage reports a return window, and disagrees with itself.**

| Source | Reported return window | Reported premium |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting on the DOE solicitation (via search summary, Argus/Reuters cited) | 1 Nov 2026 to 30 Sep 2028 | 18-22% |
| Plainview Energy deep-dive | "late 2026-2029" | 18-24% |
| DOE, on its own RFP | *(none stated)* | 26% |

Three sources, three windows, three premiums, and **not one of them cites a
document**. The Plainview piece was fetched and checked specifically for a
citation - no solicitation number, no SAM.gov notice, no FedConnect posting.

**What this establishes, and what it does not.** The return terms exist, and
they exist *in the solicitation and the resulting contracts* - not in any DOE
press release, which is why three site searches across two weeks found nothing.
That is a specific, named, identifiable class of federal record.

It is emphatically **not** a denominator. Slate v1.1 section 1 needs the volume
due for physical return on or before 2027-12-31 **per agreement**. An aggregate
programme window, reported second-hand and inconsistently, is not that, and it
is not being treated as that. **No substitution is made here.**

One thing it does settle: the reported window opens 1 November 2026 and runs
into 2028, which straddles the resolution date. If that reporting is even
roughly right, some crude is due before 2027-12-31 and the zero-denominator
guard would not fire on the facts - it would fire on the *unavailability of the
record*. Those are different reasons for the same disposition and the
annotation should say which one applied.

**Consequence for the FOIA decision, which is Mica's.** The case is stronger
than it was on 08-20, when the target was "whatever DOE has". The target is now
the 2026 SPR exchange solicitation and the resulting award contracts, held by
the Office of Petroleum Reserves. Against that, one honest caution: per-contract
delivery schedules are exactly the material agencies withhold under FOIA
Exemption 4 as confidential commercial information. A request may return the
solicitation's general terms - which are already reported - while redacting the
per-agreement volumes that P1 actually needs. Worth filing, in my view, because
the solicitation's stated return obligations may themselves be releasable; worth
filing with the expectation of partial redaction rather than a clean answer.

**Provenance:** 2026-08-21, machine BLEEDING-HEART. `energy.gov` and
`plainview-energy.com` both fetched directly and read. The DOE RFP article is
primary and was read in full; everything in the table above except the DOE row
is SECONDARY and is labelled as such. Nothing from this attempt is eligible to
serve as the anchor.

### What this means for P1

Slate v1.1 §1 anchors the denominator to "the first schedule DOE published for
that agreement, whenever published". If no such schedule is publicly
retrievable, then at resolution the denominator cannot be established from the
public record, and **P1 resolves UNSCORED** — the same disposition its
zero-denominator guard already provides for.

That is a legitimate outcome and it does not get worked around. Specifically:

- **Do not substitute an amended or current schedule for the initial one.**
  Deferral is the thesis's own predicted behaviour, so a later schedule would
  bias the denominator toward a YES for the null. The anchor exists precisely to
  stop that.
- **Do not reconstruct a denominator from EIA delivery data.** Deliveries are
  the numerator; inferring the denominator from them is circular.

### Remaining avenues after two attempts

**FOIA to DOE's Office of Petroleum Reserves** is now the only route with a real
chance of producing the initial schedule. It is also the only one with weeks of
lead time, so the decision to file is time-sensitive in a way the search no
longer is. **Mica's call** — it puts his name on a federal records request.

**SAM.gov with a registration key.** The public API returned 404 without one and
the search UI is a JavaScript application. A key is free; whether award notices
carry return schedules at all is unknown.

**Contemporaneous trade press** (Argus, Platts, Reuters) may have reported return
terms when the agreements were signed. A secondary source would have to be
labelled as such and could not carry the same weight as a DOE publication for a
pre-registered denominator.

If none produces the initial schedule, record P1 as UNSCORED with this section
as the reason, rather than scoring it on a substitute denominator.
