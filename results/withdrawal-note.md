# Withdrawal of the correlated-drain claim

**Date:** 2026-07-31, ratified 2026-08-01. **Status:** written before any contact
with real data.

---

## The claim, and its withdrawal

Hypothesis [H], from Index Spec v0.2 §0: *US collective buffers have been
draining in increasingly correlated fashion over 2000–2026.*

**That claim is withdrawn.** The instrument built to test it does not identify
the quantity it was meant to measure. Every alternative design examined has the
same problem, and the reason is common to all of them, so the withdrawal is not
expected to survive a better implementation.

## What this is a statement about

This is a statement about a measuring instrument, not about the world.

The test cannot tell rising co-movement apart from a shared trend that bends.
That is a fact about the statistic. It is **not** evidence that buffers have
failed to drain together, and nothing here should be read as showing the
hypothesis false. What can be said is narrower: this design could not have told
the difference, so any answer it produced would not have been worth having.

The claim is withdrawn because it is unsupported, not because it is refuted.
Those are different, and the difference is not a hedge — an unsupported claim
does not get to stay in the framework in weaker language.

## Not identified, which is worse than underpowered

The design is also severely underpowered, which on its own would be grounds for
reporting the claim untestable. The binding defect is worse than that, and the
distinction matters.

The headline statistic is the change in mean pairwise correlation between
first-differenced domain series. Differencing is meant to strip out shared
secular trend, leaving only co-movement of the deviations. **It does that only
if the shared trend is linear.**

A shared depletion path with **curvature** survives differencing. Its first
difference varies over time, and it is identical across every domain — which is
the definition of a common factor. Simulated with identical depletion paths in
every domain, fully independent noise, and **no rise whatsoever in genuine
co-movement**:

| Curvature of the shared path | Realized Δρ | Confirm rate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (linear) | +0.001 | 0.129 |
| 1 | +0.026 | 0.143 |
| 2 | +0.082 | 0.169 |
| 3 | +0.166 | 0.216 |
| 5 | **+0.358** | **0.330** |

seed `2026072802`. Full description in

A second mechanism compounds it: the late block spans a wider index range than
the early block, so a shared component whose variance grows over time
contributes more variance to the late block. The contrast comes out positive
**by construction**, before any real co-movement exists.

### Why no redesign rescues it

The mechanism is a property of **the differencing family**. Every candidate
examined — C-A through C-E, spanning annual and quarterly frequency, endpoint
contrasts and all-window trend statistics, four domains and eight — computes
correlations of differenced series, and a shared curved path inflates the
measured correlation in all of them.

**The precise scope of that claim, stated honestly:** D1b was measured
empirically for the frozen design (C-A) only; there is no per-candidate
breakdown. The extension to the other candidates is a mechanism argument, and it
is strongest for the endpoint contrasts, which compare two windows and are
directly exposed to a trend whose curvature differs between them. For the
rolling-trend candidates (C-D, C-E) the inflation would have to *change across
the sample* to bias the trend statistic, which a shared quadratic path does only
partially. The magnitude for those candidates is untested.

This does not rescue the design. The withdrawal rests on the identification
failure demonstrated for the frozen design itself, and independently on the
power and calibration findings below, which are measured per candidate. But
"no candidate escapes it" would be stronger than the evidence carries, and the
correct statement is that **the mechanism applies to every candidate and its
magnitude is demonstrated for C-A.**

The candidate comparison is in
[`design-comparison/README.md`](design-comparison/README.md); none reached 80%
power at Δρ = 0.15 either, but that is the lesser finding.

**A system in which every buffer drains at an accelerating rate produces a
positive result from this instrument with no rise in co-movement at all.** That
is arguably the most natural reading of the thesis. The test cannot tell the
thesis's own mechanism apart from its own artifact.

## Supporting findings

Each of these would have been disclosed as a limitation. Together with the
identification failure they close the question.

**Power.** Minimum detectable effect at 80% power is **Δρ ≈ 0.52**, against
effects anyone would actually predict of 0.10–0.20. At Δρ = 0.15 the full
four-part conjunction confirms 28.7% of the time against 12.4% when nothing is
happening — a likelihood ratio of **0.81 for a non-confirmation**. A
disconfirmation would have shifted the odds by about a fifth.

**Calibration.** The corrected false-positive rate is **0.18 against a nominal
0.10**. Decomposed: +0.029 from the small-sample bootstrap, and **+0.051 from
the crisis-exclusion rule**, which under the actual calendar layout leaks
one-sidedly into the late block and biases the estimate **toward the
hypothesis**. See [`../REVIEWER-NOTES.md`](../REVIEWER-NOTES.md) errata and item 5.

**The conjunction.** C3 and C4 add 0.012–0.066 over C1 and C2 alone, once the
Dirichlet reweighting is drawn over domains with pair weight as the product, per
Spec v0.2 §6. They cannot add much more: the drop-one reruns and the reweightings
are deterministic functions of the same 28 pairwise differences that produce the
headline estimate. A robustness check computed from the same numbers as the
statistic it checks is only weakly independent of it.

## The commitment

Spec v0.2 §0, verbatim:

> *If the pre-specified analysis returns flat or domain-idiosyncratic
> cross-domain covariance over 2000–2026, the correlated-drain claim is
> withdrawn from the framework. No hedging into "not yet measurable."*

The claim is withdrawn. The data was never fetched, so no result influenced the
decision either way. It rests entirely on synthetic tests of the instrument, run
before the gate opened.

"Not identified" could be mistaken for the hedge that clause bans, so worth being
explicit. *Not yet measurable* would mean the hypothesis stands and is waiting
for a better instrument. That is not what this says. The claim is withdrawn: not
kept in weaker language, not deferred, and not available to be re-asserted over
2000–2026 by some other method without a new versioned specification, externally
timestamped like this one.

## P6 stands

P6 — the index's own out-of-sample trajectory, measured by the quarterly
companion of Spec v0.2 §7 (S8) — **remains frozen and will be scored at
resolution exactly as defined.**

It is not dropped. A prediction registered in public does not get withdrawn
because the project it belonged to changed shape, and a frozen instrument does
not get redefined because a later finding made it inconvenient. The scoring
proceeds on the original terms, on 2029-03-31.

**Disclosed caveat:** D1b applies to the quarterly companion as it applies to
every differencing-based statistic here. A RISE outcome will therefore be
consistent with rising co-movement *and* with a shared acceleration in depletion
rates, and P6 cannot distinguish them. That limitation is recorded now, before
resolution, rather than raised afterwards by whichever side it happens to suit.

## What this withdrawal does not touch

- **The descriptive layer.** That buffers are individually thin and thinning is
  a separate claim, evidenced separately, and untouched by this. It is being
  written up as an explicitly exploratory chartbook.
- **The prediction slate.** Frozen, public, and unaffected.
- **The ratchet question** — whether buffers rebuild after stress episodes or
  step permanently lower — which is a different question with a different
  statistic, and is being pre-specified separately before any data contact.
